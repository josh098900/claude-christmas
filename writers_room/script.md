# SEEN FROM SPACE: the script

*A wordless film. Running time 2:48 including the end card. Timecodes are approximate; frame-exact timings live in `engine/src/film.ts` and `writers_room/shot_list.md`.*

---

### 0:00 · THE ONLY DARK HOUSE

EXT. A SNOWY ENGLISH TOWN, DUSK. We glide low over snow-heavy rooftops. Chimneys smoke and every house is strung with coloured lights. The camera drifts down into one terraced street, where every house twinkles except one in the middle, dark and blue.

A small brass bell hangs by its front door, frosted solid. It doesn't move.

INT. GRANDAD'S FRONT ROOM. GRANDAD (75, tall, stooped, cardigan, big gentle hands) stands at the dark window holding a mug that has gone cold. The neighbours' lights paint faint colours on the glass, though not on him. A clock ticks.

The armchair beside the fire is empty. A dusty-rose cardigan is folded over its arm.

On the mantelpiece sits a framed photo. GRANDMA and GRANDAD, younger, stand in front of this house blazing with lights. She is laughing and pointing at the sky. He crouches by the front door with a bulb between his fingers, looking up at her.

*(Music: the motif on celesta, alone. Its last note never comes.)*

### 0:17 · NELL DECIDES

NELL (9, big red coat, mustard bobble hat, two short plaits, green wellies, very expressive eyebrows) sits cross-legged on the rug and looks up at the photo. Then she looks at the window, at the twinkling street and at Grandad's back. Then at the photo again.

Her eyebrows go sad, then knit, then set. Decision.

Her eyes slide to the back door. A key hangs on a hook, with a paper tag in Grandma's handwriting: SHED.

### 0:27 · THE SHED

EXT. GRANDAD'S GARDEN, NIGHT. The shed door creaks open. A torch beam cuts through drifting dust onto a stack of boxes, labelled in Grandma's looping capitals: LIGHTS — FRONT. LIGHTS — ROOF (careful!).

Nell's mitten wipes the dust off one label.

She tiptoes the boxes across the snowy garden on her sledge, past the window where Grandad sits with his back to her, and out of the gate. Then she's a tiny figure hauling a sledge piled high under the streetlamps, all the way home.

### 0:39 · THE OPERATION

INT. NELL'S BEDROOM, NIGHTS. A sign on her door, hand-lettered: KEEP OUT. ESPECIALLY SANTA. The door shuts and light glows under it.

What follows plays like a heist montage. A desk drowned in knotted light strings, lit by a torch and the warm glow of a tablet. Her small hands pick at the knots. On the tablet, slow two-finger typing:

> why does 1 bulb make them ALL go off

A reply appears, warm and soft and out of focus.

She tests bulbs, one by one. The dead ones go *plink* into a jam jar.

> how to find the broken bulb in xmas lights

The wall fills with diagrams joined by red string: a roof, a chimney, arrows, a big X. On the whiteboard a tally climbs: 12 … 340 … 1,206.

Downstairs, MUM and DAD look up at the ceiling, *thump*, then at each other. Worried.

> how many lights do you need to be seen from space

Nell's face glows blue and warm. Her eyebrows are fully up.

Nights pass. The jar of dead bulbs fills, the untangled strings pile up and the Advent calendar doors open. We're sure of it now: she is building a Santa trap.

### 1:04 · THE TURN

She types slowly, and this time we read every letter:

> i need them on the roof but im not allowed up ladders

For the first time we can read the reply. It's plain and short:

> Good rule. This bit needs more people. Who lives on the street?

*(Mum is beside her now, reading over her shoulder. She smiles.)*

Nell and Mum look up at her wall, and we pull back with them. Every string on the wall leads to one place in the middle: a child's drawing of Grandad's house blazing with lights, with a stick-Grandma pointing at the sky.

It was never a trap.

She grabs her hat.

### 1:16 · KNOCKING ON DOORS

EXT. GRANDAD'S STREET, BLUE HOUR. Nell marches up the street with her drawing rolled under her arm, past his dark house.

Knock. An OLD LADY with a little dog in a jumper looks at the drawing, then across the street at the dark house. She nods, and the dog's tail goes.

At the CORNER SHOP, the SHOPKEEPER looks, then nods, then thumps a box of bulbs onto the counter.

TWO TEENAGERS on a wall look at each other. Fist bump.

A YOUNG DAD with a BABY in a sling. The baby grabs the drawing. Dad nods.

Then the big door. The LADDER MAN (huge, moustache, scowl) reads the drawing without a flicker. He looks across the street at Grandad, alone in his dark window. Something passes over his face. He shuts the door.

Nell's eyebrows sink. Snow settles on her hat.

The door opens again. The end of a very long ladder comes out first. He nods, once.

### 1:34 · CHRISTMAS EVE

Mum walks Grandad off arm-in-arm towards the carol service and the lit church spire. Behind his back she gives the street a wink.

The street springs into action. Doors open and neighbours pour out with ladders, boxes and torches, and the torch beams criss-cross through falling snow. The ladder man goes up the big ladder. The teenagers pass strings up. Small hands pass to big hands pass to the roof. Nell runs the operation from the pavement with her clipboard. The young dad plugs it in, and the old lady holds the torch.

### 1:49 · THE DEAD SWITCH

Grandad and Mum walk home under the streetlamps. The whole street is dark, every single house. Grandad slows.

In front of his house a crowd of neighbours stands silhouetted, waiting. Nell steps forward with a stack of cue cards.

> GRANDMA ALWAYS SAID

Grandad's eyebrows move.

> IT SHOULD BE SEEN FROM SPACE

> SO…

She flips the big switch. *Click.*

Nothing.

The music has gone. There's only wind. Faces in the dark. Nell's face falls slowly, all the way. This is the worst moment in the film, and we hold on it.

### 2:08 · GRANDAD FINISHES IT

Grandad kneels beside her, slowly, the way old knees do. He takes the string in his big hands and walks his fingers along it, bulb by bulb, towards the front door. Each bulb is a note of the motif on solo piano. His fingers stop at the bulb where the missing note should be, right by the door. He wiggles it, and knows.

He looks at her, takes her small hand and puts it on the bulb.

She twists.

**THE HOUSE BLAZES.** Red, green, blue, amber and white, every window frame, every gutter, the roof, the chimney. The missing note lands on that exact frame, and the whole orchestra blooms behind it.

Down the street, house after house comes back on, one by one, like a lit fuse.

Nell looks up, beaming, and points at the sky, exactly like Grandma in the photo. Grandad sees it. He laughs for the first time in the film, a real one, and pulls her in. By the door the little bell, thawed, tinkles in the wind.

### 2:27 · SEEN FROM SPACE

We rise along her pointing arm. Rooftops, then the street ablaze, then the town, then through cloud, then the dark curve of the Earth at night, scattered with city lights. One tiny warm dot is brighter than everything around it.

### 2:37 · CODA

INT. GRANDAD'S FRONT ROOM, LATER. The armchair is no longer empty. Nell is asleep in it under Grandma's cardigan, with the tablet on her lap:

> it worked. everyone helped.

Grandad sits beside her. The window behind them is ablaze with colour. The clock ticks, and the motif plays complete, softly, once.

### 2:43 · END CARD

Night blue. *Some problems need a whole street.* and **Claude** in clean type.
